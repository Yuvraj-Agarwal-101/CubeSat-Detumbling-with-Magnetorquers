\section{Conclusions}
The integrated model combines a real idealized orbital magnetic field,
normalized B-dot feedback, vector-norm actuator clipping, quaternion
attitude dynamics, and scratch RK4. Independent physics checks, smooth
convergence tests, saturation-aware and high-spin refinement, and separate
event-output checks support the accepted $0.25\ \mathrm{s}$ timestep within
the verified scope. The canonical $0.1\ \mathrm{A\,m^2}$ baseline actuator
is unsaturated; the broader tested regimes include saturation.

The final targeted engineering study contains 16 real cases: 15 recoveries,
one censored deadline failure, and no invalid simulations. It does not show
that every tested case meets the six-orbit requirement. The failed case is
geometry 0 at $30\ \mathrm{deg/s}$, gain $3\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm{kg\,m^2/s}$,
and limit $0.03\ \mathrm{A\,m^2}$. Its recovery time is unknown within the
study horizon rather than set equal to the deadline.

The matched comparisons show greater difficulty at higher initial spin,
a strong benefit from the tested tenfold gain increase, and a substantial
actuator benefit when clipping is active. For the high-spin, higher-gain
case, a tenfold larger dipole limit reduces recovery time by 61.00\%.
For the low-spin, lower-gain cases, larger limits have no measured effect
because even the smallest bound is inactive. Recovery times across the
four common geometries differ by a factor of 3.89, emphasizing the role of
initial attitude, spin direction, and orbital phase.

These conclusions apply to the tested parameter combinations under the
stated assumed inertia and idealized field. They do not establish a global
optimum, a population recovery probability, eventual impossibility for a
censored trajectory, or recovery for every CubeSat configuration. The
planned 108-case pilot and 1152-case production sweep were not completed;
production-sweep readiness is not claimed. The saved real histories,
configuration hashes, tested revision, and reproducible figures provide
the evidence for the submitted targeted study.
